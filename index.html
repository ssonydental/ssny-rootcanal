Signs You May Need a Root Canal.

Six signs the nerve inside a tooth may be infected. Only an exam and X-ray can confirm it, and that’s the first step:

  • Urgent

    Lingering pain

    Pain that lingers after hot or cold, instead of fading in a few seconds.

  • Urgent

    Swelling or a bump

    Swelling or a pimple-like bump on the gum near the tooth.

  • Urgent

    Pain that wakes you up

    A throbbing ache that gets worse at night or lying down.

  • Pain when biting

    Pressure or sharp pain when you chew or tap the tooth.

  • A darkened tooth

    One tooth that has turned grey or darker than its neighbors.

  • A cracked or deeply decayed tooth

    A crack or cavity deep enough to reach the nerve.

● Quick Answer ●

What Is a Root Canal?

A root canal (also called endodontic treatment) removes the infected or inflamed nerve tissue, the pulp, from inside a tooth, then cleans, disinfects and seals the root canals so the natural tooth can stay in place. It treats the cause of a toothache rather than masking it. At Smile Services of New York in Manhasset, NY, a typical visit takes 60 to 90 minutes under local anesthetic, and most patients return to work the same day.

● Why It Hurts ●

Why a Toothache Needs a Root Canal: The Infected Pulp Is What Hurts.

Inside every tooth is a soft core of nerve and blood vessels called the pulp. When decay or a crack lets bacteria reach it, the pulp becomes inflamed and infected. That’s the throbbing, the sensitivity, the pain that won’t quit.

A root canal removes the infected pulp, disinfects the canals, and seals the tooth. The pain goes with it, and the tooth stays.

● Don't Wait ●

The Longer It Waits, the Worse It Gets.

An infected nerve doesn’t heal on its own. Left alone, the infection can spread to the bone at the root tip and cause swelling, an abscess, or the loss of the tooth. Painkillers buy a few hours. They don’t fix what’s underneath.

Keeping your natural tooth is almost always the better long-term option, and the sooner it’s treated, the simpler it is.

● Root Canal FAQ ●

Root Canal Questions, Answered.

+Does a root canal hurt?

No more than a filling for most patients. The pain you feel now is the infection. With local anesthetic you feel pressure, not pain, and the procedure removes the source of the toothache.

+How long does a root canal take?

Most root canals at Smile Services of New York in Manhasset are completed in a single visit. The number of visits depends on the tooth and is confirmed at your exam.

+How do I know if I need a root canal or just a filling?

A filling treats decay that has not reached the nerve. A root canal is needed when the pulp inside the tooth is infected or inflamed: lingering pain after hot or cold, pain when biting, swelling or a bump on the gum, a tooth that has darkened, or pain that wakes you at night. Only an exam and X-ray can confirm which one you need.

+Is a root canal a dental emergency?

It can be. Swelling, a throbbing toothache that keeps you up at night, or a bump on the gum are signs of an active infection and should be evaluated the same day. We offer same-day evaluations for tooth pain in Manhasset.

+Will I need a crown after a root canal?

Usually, yes. A crown protects the treated tooth long-term and is placed at a follow-up visit. Your dentist confirms what is recommended for your tooth.

+Wouldn’t pulling the tooth be easier?

Keeping your natural tooth is almost always the better long-term option. An extraction leaves a gap that usually needs an implant or bridge later, which costs more time and money than saving the tooth.

+How much does a root canal cost in Manhasset?

Cost depends on which tooth is treated and whether a crown is needed. You get your price before treatment starts, payment is made at the visit. Financing options are available.

+Will antibiotics cure an infected tooth without a root canal?

No. Antibiotics can calm swelling for a short time, but they cannot reach and remove the infected pulp inside the tooth. The infection returns until the source is treated with a root canal or an extraction.
